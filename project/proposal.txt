Title: Shear locking in thin beams and how mixed finite elements remove it

Article: D. N. Arnold, "Discretization by finite elements of a model
parameter dependent problem," Numerische Mathematik 37 (1981), 405-421.
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386420   (also indexed at https://eudml.org/doc/132740)

Proposal:
Arnold studies a clamped Timoshenko beam of thickness t and shows that the
standard finite element method loses accuracy as t -> 0 (shear locking),
while a family of mixed methods, which includes the reduced-integration
elements used in engineering practice, is quasi-optimal uniformly in t.
I will present the main error estimate, explaining locking in the language
of the course as a Cea bound whose constant degenerates as t -> 0, and why
the mixed formulation removes that dependence. I will also write a short 1D
code comparing full and reduced integration as t decreases.

Title: DistMesh: mesh generation from signed distance functions

Article: P.-O. Persson and G. Strang, "A simple mesh generator in MATLAB,"
SIAM Review 46 (2004), 329-345.  https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036144503429121

Proposal:
Persson and Strang generate triangular meshes by treating the mesh as a truss
whose bars push nodes apart until force equilibrium, with the domain given
implicitly by a signed distance function. I will explain the algorithm
(force law, Delaunay retriangulation, projection of boundary nodes), run the
code on several domains, and study mesh quality and its effect on finite
element error. I will also examine what happens when the level set is not a
true distance function, where boundary nodes can be left off the boundary.

Title: A complete finite element solver in 50 lines of MATLAB

Article: J. Alberty, C. Carstensen, S. A. Funken, "Remarks around 50 lines of
Matlab: short finite element implementation," Numerical Algorithms 20 (1999),
117-137.  https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1019155918070

Proposal:
Alberty, Carstensen and Funken give a one-page MATLAB code for P1 triangles
and Q1 parallelograms solving elliptic problems with mixed Dirichlet/Neumann
boundary conditions on unstructured meshes. I will explain how each line maps
to the Galerkin formulation (local stiffness matrices, assembly, boundary
conditions), run the code, and verify the expected convergence rates,
extending it to a nonlinear or time-dependent example from the paper.

Title: Mixed-mode cohesive zone elements under compression: Camanho's
bilinear law, its finite element implementation, and its limits for a
confined natural fracture

Article: P. P. Camanho, C. G. Davila, M. F. de Moura, "Numerical simulation
of mixed-mode progressive delamination in composite materials," Journal of
Composite Materials 37 (2003), 1415-1438.
https://doi.org/10.1177/0021998303034505
(Journal version of NASA/TM-2002-211737, the report MOOSE cites for its
bilinear mixed-mode law.)

Proposal:
Camanho, Davila and de Moura model interface fracture with zero-thickness
decohesion elements governed by a bilinear mixed-mode traction-separation
law: a quadratic stress criterion for damage onset, the Benzeggagh-Kenane
criterion for propagation, and a single irreversible damage variable. I will
derive the finite element formulation, in which an interface integral of
traction times the jump of the test function enters the weak form, and show
how the damage evolves in pure opening, pure shear and compression-shear
using single-element MOOSE simulations from my ongoing CDM+CZM work on
natural-fracture reactivation in shale. I will then focus on compression,
where the law assumes that normal pressure affects neither damage onset nor
toughness, show how the implementation of its closed-interface branch changes
the dissipated shear energy, and outline the extensions a confined fracture
needs: friction acting on the damaged part of the interface and
compression-enhanced shear toughness.
